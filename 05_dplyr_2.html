The result is still grouped by act_use_cat. Later steps then work within each type, which may not be what you want.

Add .groups = "drop" to summarise(), or call ungroup(), when you are done with groups.

Practice: a price index

Using avg_price, compute a price index for each act_use_cat: the average price divided by the average price in 2010.

Answer

price_index <- avg_price |>
  group_by(act_use_cat) |>
  mutate(index_2010 = avg_price / avg_price[sale_year == 2010]) |>
  ungroup()

price_index |> filter(sale_year %in% c(2010, 2020))

Row positions are fragile

An AI might write nth(avg_price, 11) for “the 2010 value”, because 2010 is the 11th year:

avg_price |>
  arrange(desc(sale_year)) |>
  group_by(act_use_cat) |>
  summarise(nth_11 = sale_year[11])
# A tibble: 2 × 2
  act_use_cat nth_11
  <chr>        <dbl>
1 S/F Res       2016
2 Strata Res    2016

After a different sort, or with a missing year, the 11th row is no longer 2010. Select the year by its value, as in avg_price[sale_year == 2010].

Practice: annual growth

Compute the annual growth rate of avg_price for each act_use_cat: (this year − last year) / last year.

Answer

avg_price |>
  arrange(act_use_cat, sale_year) |>
  group_by(act_use_cat) |>
  mutate(growth = (avg_price - lag(avg_price)) / lag(avg_price)) |>
  ungroup() |>
  filter(sale_year %in% 2023:2025)
# A tibble: 6 × 4
  act_use_cat sale_year avg_price   growth
  <chr>           <dbl>     <dbl>    <dbl>
1 S/F Res          2023  2004597. -0.0266 
2 S/F Res          2024  2038861.  0.0171 
3 S/F Res          2025  1968313. -0.0346 
4 Strata Res       2023   812047. -0.00646
5 Strata Res       2024   833132.  0.0260

lag() uses row order, so sort by year first.
