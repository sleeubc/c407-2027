Preparation
Topic: Find and present interesting or useful statistical facts using the Canadian Census Public Use Micro File (PUMF) data. You may choose any topic as long as it utilizes this dataset.


Deliverables:


Slides: A two-page slide deck. The first page must feature a single figure; the second page should explain why the figure is significant or useful.


R Script: Submit the R script used to generate your figure. The script must be configured to save the figure as a PNG file in the temp folder within C407 folder. The file name has to be ind_<student no>.png where you replace <student no> with your student number.


Presentation Details
Dates: Presentations will take place on Feb 24 and 26.


Deadline: All slides must be submitted by Feb 22 at 11:59 PM.


Scheduling: If you have a conflict with one of the presentation dates, please notify me by Feb 5.


Format: Each student will have approximately 4 minutes to present. Reading from a script is not allowed.


Evaluation Criteria


How interesting is your finding?

Imagine you are sending your figure to a magazine targeting university students, professors, and policymakers. Would they be interested in your findings? How would you motivate your presentation to make it interesting to them?

Your finding provides important information for social issues and has policy implications.

Your finding improves our understanding of the society. It is somewhat surprising at first but makes sense with your explanation.


How proficient are you in data processing and creating a data figure?

There is no mistake or error in your codes. (Make sure to account for WEIGHT and to remove the special values when necessary.)

The figure is effective in delivering the key point and is visually pleasing.


Presentation. Delivery.

Eye contact. Voice volume. Speed. (Eye contact is most important among these. The other two usually come naturally as long as you make good eye contact with the audience.)

Common Pitfalls to Avoid
Based on previous years, please be mindful of the following:

Complexity: Avoid overly complex figures that distract from your main message.

Image Quality: Do not use screenshots; they appear blurry. Use the ggsave() function to export high-quality images.

Data Accuracy: Ensure you account for the WEIGHT variable in the PUMF data and deal with the “special values” properly.
