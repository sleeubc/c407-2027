COMM 407: Group Presentation Guidelines
1. Key Dates & Deadlines
March 10 (7:00 PM): Research question signup sheet opens.
March 17 (11:59 PM): Deadline to notify the instructor of any scheduling conflicts.
March 19: Presentation dates announced.
March 28 (11:59 PM): Final Submission Deadline. All groups must submit slides and R scripts (regardless of presentation date).
March 31 & April 2: Presentation Days.
2. Presentation Logistics
Format: 10-minute presentation followed by a 3-minute Q&A.
Data analysis visuals:
Include 6–7 figures, tables, or maps.
Requirement: At least one map is required.
Originality: All data analysis visuals must be original results from your own data analysis performed in R.
Slides: No specific slide limit. Focus on visual clarity and minimize heavy blocks of text.
3. Research Topics & Data
Scope: Questions must relate to real estate, urban economics, or urban public policy broadly interpreted (e.g., housing, transportation, crime, segregation, education, public health).

Signup Process: Only one group per research question. Registration is first-come, first-served on the shared research signup spreadsheet. Changes to a topic are allowed as long as they do not overlap with existing registrations on the spreadsheet

Avoid: Simple correlations between proximity to features (e.g., Skytrain stations, parks) and housing prices, as causal effects are difficult to estimate.

Data: Any publicly available data + any datasets used in class (e.g., BCA)

4. Required Deliverables
Presentation Slides: Submitted as a PPTX or PDF.
R Script: * Must produce all figures and tables used in the presentation.
Must save the primary figure as a PNG in the C407/temp folder.
Naming Convention: grp_<group no>_<figure name>.png (e.g., grp_05_crime_rates.png).
5. Evaluation Criteria
Your presentation will be judged based on the following three pillars:

The “Interest” Factor

Policy Implications: Does the finding address an important social issue?
Insight: Does it improve our understanding of society? (The best findings are often surprising at first but logical once explained).
Audience Appeal: Is the topic motivated well enough to interest university students, professors, and policymakers?
Technical Proficiency

Coding Accuracy: Error-free R scripts.
Visualization Quality: Figures should be visually pleasing and effectively deliver a key point. Use ggsave() for high-quality images (no screenshots).
Delivery & Storytelling

Engagement: Strong eye contact, appropriate volume, and steady pacing.
Structure: Motivate the question, provide clear interpretations, and offer key takeaways.
Interesting and clear storytelling
No scripts: Reading from a script or a computer monitor is strictly prohibited. You may use one or two memory cards for key points, but you must maintain active eye contact with your audience.
